Pro features

More of what Pro can see

New to the terms? Explore our interactive cable diagram to see how charging, data and video use the parts inside a USB-C cable.

Every one of these inspects a connection in more detail than the free app can. The full Free and Pro comparison follows below.

Cable history

Name a cable, recognise it on later connections, and keep a timeline of how it performs.

Negotiation Diagnostics

Find out which part of the connection is holding it back: your Mac’s port, the cable or the connected device.

Technical detail

“Negotiated” means the speed or power the connected devices have agreed to use. WhatCable compares this with the reported capabilities of each part, and identifies a limit when the available evidence supports it.

Display Diagnostics

Find out why your screen is not as sharp or smooth as expected. Compare its picture settings with what the screen and its connection can support.

Technical detail

Resolution describes picture detail; refresh rate is how often the picture updates. Display Diagnostics compares these with the monitor’s reported modes and the DisplayPort connection’s lanes and rate. It accounts for Display Stream Compression (DSC), which fits the picture into less bandwidth, and adapter information when available. A lower link rate can be appropriate for the selected picture setting.

Live power metering

See how much power your Mac reports through each port, with readings updated every two seconds.

Technical detail

Watts (W) measure power, amps (A) measure current and volts (V) measure voltage. Where live per-port readings are unavailable, WhatCable shows the agreed maximum instead. That limit is not the same as the power being used at that moment.

PD contract inspector

See which charging options your charger offers and which one your Mac is using. This helps explain why a powerful charger may be charging at a lower rate.

Technical detail

USB Power Delivery (PD) is how compatible devices agree charging power. The agreement is called a power contract. Power Data Objects (PDOs) describe the available charging profiles. The inspector shows voltage, current and available profiles, and flags capability mismatches.

Cable resistance estimation

Estimate how much the cable resists the flow of electricity. This can help you investigate power loss while testing a cable.

Technical detail

The estimate uses multiple power samples and is shown in milliohms (mΩ), a unit of electrical resistance. It is an estimate, not a direct measurement or proof that a cable is worn.

Port health counters

Look for repeated disconnects, restarts and electrical errors reported by a port. Changes over time can help you investigate an unreliable connection.

Technical detail

Counters can include attach/detach events, hard resets, power-role swaps and internal communication or switching errors. A power-role swap changes which device supplies power. These are reported events, not a complete lifetime wear record or proof that the cable caused a fault.

PD event trace

Follow what happened when you connected a cable: the charging setup, any interruptions and the final agreement.

Technical detail

The USB Power Delivery event trace shows the protocol messages and events reported for a port, including attachment, contract negotiation and hard resets that restart power negotiation.

DP Alt Mode details

See how the picture travels from your Mac to the screen, along with the display information your Mac can read.

Technical detail

DisplayPort Alt Mode carries DisplayPort video through USB-C. The details include lane count (the signal paths in use), link rate (their speed), and whether video travels directly or through a Thunderbolt tunnel. EDID is the monitor’s identity and capabilities data, which can include its model, manufacturer and year.

Raw VDO identity

Inspect the exact identity data reported by the cable and connected device, beyond the usual summary.

Technical detail

Vendor Defined Objects (VDOs) are structured blocks of identity and capability information. SOP addresses the connected device; SOP′ addresses cable electronics. Keeping them separate helps distinguish the device’s claims from the cable’s.

Liquid detection

Check the liquid-detection state reported for a USB-C port when investigating an intermittent connection.

Technical detail

WhatCable displays the available liquid-detection controller state, also called LDCM. This is a report from the Mac’s hardware, not a physical inspection of the port.

Power monitor window

Watch a chart of power and voltage over time while you try a charger or cable. Keep the window open beside your work.

Technical detail

The chart shows the available per-port readings. Where the Mac does not provide live power draw, the displayed negotiated maximum is the agreed limit, not measured consumption.

CLI monitor mode

whatcable --monitor streams live power and cable state to your terminal. Pipe it, script it, log it.

TUI dashboard

A live full-screen terminal view of ports, power, and Thunderbolt. Three screens, updates every 2 seconds. For the SSH and tmux crowd: whatcable --dashboard.

Widget power sparkline

Desktop widget shows a power chart alongside cable status, updated periodically by macOS on its own schedule. Glance at your desktop and see charging trends without opening anything.

CC advertisement level

Common questions

Does this work on Intel Macs?
No. WhatCable requires Apple Silicon (M1 or later). The deep IOKit data sources Pro uses are only exposed by Apple's own port controllers, which aren't present on Intel Macs with third-party Thunderbolt chips.
What macOS version do I need?
macOS 14 Sonoma or later. The free app has the same requirement.
How does activation work?
After purchase, your licence key arrives by email. Right-click WhatCable in the menu bar, click Licence, paste the key, and pro features unlock immediately. The app validates once on activation and once per launch, with a 30-day offline grace period if you're without internet.
Can I use one key on multiple Macs?
Yes. One key works on up to 2 Macs. Need more? Buy additional licences, or ask about volume licensing for 10+ Macs.
Is the free version still available?
Yes, always. The free app is open source, on GitHub, and installable via Homebrew. Everything that is free today stays free. Pro adds the deeper diagnostics on top.
