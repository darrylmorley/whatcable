Read the label. Check the connection.

This library collects identity-chip reports from WhatCable users. A familiar name or matching code can help identify a cable, but is not a unique serial number or proof of authenticity.

A missing manufacturer or certificate alone does not mean a cable is faulty. A compatible passive cable may also carry newer signals faster than its older label suggests. WhatCable compares available reports across the port, cable and connected device to help explain your actual connection.

Speed, charging and technical terms

Mbps / Gbps describe data capacity; W (watts) describes power. More charging power does not mean faster file transfers. The charger and device must also support the power being requested.

Passive cables do not boost or rebuild data signals; active cables contain electronics that do. Both can have an identity chip.

VID and PID identify the reported vendor and product. VDO is the encoded capability data. XID is a certification identifier, not proof of Thunderbolt certification. “Unregistered” means the vendor ID was not found in the list used here.

Power values are preserved from the reports, including unusual combinations. A declared voltage/current pair alone does not establish compliance. A 50 V, 5 A cable rating permits a maximum nominal USB Power Delivery contract of 48 V × 5 A = 240 W.
